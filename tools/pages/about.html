About

A common-sense effort for the headwaters

The vast and mountainous expanse of northern British Columbia is home to some of the most important headwaters on the continent — the Skeena, the Nass, the Stikine, the Taku, and the Liard. These living lands and waters support an incredible array of wildlife, sustain ancient communities, and face decisions that will shape BC for decades.

Northern Headwaters exists to help those decisions go well: to protect these living systems while keeping the country open, the water clean, and the jobs long-lasting. We foster collaboration among rural communities, First Nations, and outdoor people to back a protection plan that balances ecological health with economic prosperity.

Guiding principles

Three words we keep coming back to

Protection

Safeguard the natural habitats of rivers, fish, and wildlife, ensuring their prosperity and abundance for future generations.

Access

Support continued access to backcountry areas for recreation, hunting, and fishing — fostering a deep connection with the land. Humans belong here.

Certainty

Advocate for clear guidelines for land use, promoting responsible development and honest stewardship of natural resources.

What we are — and aren't

Read this before you label us

We are

Practical. Place-based. Focused on outcomes — access that lasts, rules people can see, continuity over boom-and-bust. A project of the Strong Livelihoods Society, whose directors have lived and worked in northern BC for decades.

We are not

A political party. A fundraising platform dressed as a movement. A call to end use of the land. We will not lecture you, we will not work to exclude you from the country — hunting, fishing and access stay — and we will not embarrass you for living here.

A strong livelihood is not just a job — it's continuity. Livelihoods endure only when the places that support them continue to function. That's the whole argument, and the water carries it: protect the source, and everything downstream — runs, herds, paycheques — has a chance.
