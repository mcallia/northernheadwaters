The issues file

Coming explainers, in brief

The issues we're covering next — each gets the full treatment (video + sourced brief). The one-paragraph versions, meanwhile:

Electrification & the transmission build-out

New mines need big power, and big power needs corridors. The proposed North Coast Transmission Line — with claims of tens of billions in enabled economic activity — would reshape where industrial development lands across the northwest. The question isn't wires vs. no wires; it's whether corridors follow the plan or drive it. DRAFT — full sourced brief in production

Trucks, roads & access creep

Every project brings roads, and roads outlive projects. Highway 37 already carries haul traffic alongside hunters and residents; each new spur changes where pressure lands on game and water. The plan's answer: defined footprints, and reclamation that actually happens. DRAFT — full sourced brief in production

Mountain caribou & habitat

Seven caribou herds range the Kaska planning area alone, and caribou are the first to feel roads, cutblocks and seismic lines. Habitat protection at the headwaters scale is the difference between managing herds and writing eulogies for them — and it's a fraction of the cost of emergency measures like wolf culls. DRAFT — full sourced brief in production; wolf-cull explainer video scripted

Fracking & gas pipelines

Pipeline corridors from the northeast gas fields to tidewater cross headwaters country, and revived LNG routes have put old rights-of-way back on the table. Where they cross salmon tributaries — and who decides — belongs in the same plan as everything else. DRAFT — route and status details to be confirmed by the team before publication

Contamination from existing & legacy mines

The Tulsequah Chief has leaked acid into the Taku watershed for some 65 years; the cleanup plan is still in draft. That's the strongest argument in the region for two things: real monitoring at working mines, and real bonding so closure doesn't become the public's problem. Sources: BC Gov, The Narwhal.

Briefs marked DRAFT are placeholders for team review — numbers and status get verified before any of this leaves the password wall.
